# 05. Appointment-based business platforms: feature inventory

Research date: 2026-11-06. Products: Fresha, Vagaro, Mangomint, Square Appointments, Boulevard.
Method: official help centers, pricing pages, feature pages and launch posts, supplemented by third-party pricing guides and community threads for pain points. Every row cites a source and is labeled OBSERVED (read directly on the cited page) or INFERRED (deduced from a page title, a search snippet, or a third-party source). Items that could not be verified are marked "not confirmed".

Coverage note: the shared web search budget ran out during this session, so Reddit threads (r/Hairstylist, r/Esthetics, r/smallbusiness) could not be opened, and a few help articles (Vagaro support center, Capterra review pages, Square Seller Community) returned 403 or empty pages. Those gaps are called out inline.

Tier legend: "Core" means included in the base subscription. "Add-on" means a separately billed module. "Plan: X" means it is gated to a named plan.

## Common core across these products

Every one of the five ships all of the following (OBSERVED in at least four of five; see product tables for sources):

1. Multi-staff calendar with day and week views, time blocks, appointment statuses (booked, confirmed, arrived, started, completed, no-show, canceled) and change history.
2. Service catalog with categories, duration, price, per-staff and per-location overrides, add-ons or modifiers, and some form of extra time (processing, finishing, buffer).
3. Resources (rooms, chairs, equipment) that constrain availability, gated to a higher tier only at Square.
4. Online booking page plus embeddable link or widget, Reserve with Google, Instagram and Facebook booking.
5. Booking rules: minimum lead time, maximum booking window, cancellation or reschedule cutoff, staff selection options ("any available", by gender at Fresha), slot interval.
6. No-show protection built on card capture at booking, deposits (percent or fixed) and per-service cancellation fees, with the card saved to a client wallet for later charges.
7. Waitlist with client self-join from the booking page and automated "slot opened" alerts.
8. Recurring (standing) appointments and group bookings; classes at Square and Vagaro.
9. Automated confirmations and reminders by email and SMS, with editable templates and timing offsets; two-way SMS inbox with a dedicated business number (Boulevard, Mangomint, Fresha Connect, Vagaro Connect).
10. Checkout that starts from the appointment: tips (incl. split tips), discounts, taxes, multiple payment methods, partial payments, refunds, receipts, daily register closeout.
11. Gift cards, memberships (recurring billing) and packages (prepaid units) redeemable at checkout, with client account credit as a balance type.
12. Retail products with stock tracking; suppliers and purchase orders at Boulevard and Vagaro.
13. Staff permissions by role, work hours, time off, time clock, commission rules, and payroll (in-house or via Gusto/QuickBooks).
14. Marketing: one-time email and SMS blasts to segmented audiences, with consent tracking and A2P 10DLC registration, and a set of automated lifecycle messages (rebook reminder, win-back, birthday, review request).
15. Reports: sales by service, product and staff; client records and retention; staff performance; commissions; gift card and membership liabilities.
16. An AI receptionist or concierge that answers calls or texts and books, reschedules and cancels (Fresha AI Concierge, Mangomint Mira, Boulevard Beau, Vagaro Vera; Square Assistant is older and scoped to confirmation texts).

## What is specific to appointment businesses (the module content)

This is the content an "appointments" module must own, because no other business family needs it in this shape:

- Service catalog semantics: duration, processing time (provider free), finishing time, buffer before and after, per-staff duration and price, client-specific duration, service add-ons and modifiers, "book in sequence" ordering, resource requirements, services that need a resource but no staff.
- Availability engine: staff shifts and alternating weeks, time off, business closed periods, resource capacity, lead time and booking window, slot intervals, daily booking cap, "hide some open time" (Square's Fake-it Filter), gap minimization (Boulevard Precision Scheduling), auto-assignment and dynamic reassignment (Fresha).
- Booking objects: single appointment, multi-service appointment, multi-staff appointment, group appointment, class with capacity, recurring series, requested appointment awaiting approval, waitlist entry with preferences, express booking link with expiry hold.
- Protection policies: deposit (percent or fixed, refundable-until), card capture, late-cancel fee window, no-show fee, auto-cancel if unpaid in N hours, per-service and per-client-group overrides, minimum order value, 3DS handling, and the fee-charging window (Fresha allows charging only from start time until end of day).
- Appointment lifecycle messaging: booked, rescheduled, canceled, confirmation request, reminder at N hours or days, no-show, thank you, rebook reminder (suppressed when a future appointment exists), waitlist joined and slot available, pre-care and post-care instructions.
- Front desk operations: arrival and check-in, virtual waiting room and self check-in, status icons, walk-in or no-appointment checkout, prebooking the next visit at checkout, group checkout, split checkout.
- Client record extensions: formulas and treatment notes shown at booking and checkout, allergies and patch tests, photos with markup, intake and consent forms with conditional fields and signatures, charts that copy forward, HIPAA mode for medspa.
- Staff compensation: service vs product commission, commission swap, tips to assistants, backbar fee deduction, booth rent collection.
- Appointment-specific reports: future bookings, schedule utilization, appointments and line items, rebooking and retention per staff, client waitlist report, no-show and cancellation lists.
- Marketplaces and discovery: Fresha and Vagaro marketplace listings with pay-per-new-client commissions (20% at both), Reserve with Google, Instagram booking.

## What they share with other business types

These parts are generic and should live in platform-wide modules, with the appointments module only contributing triggers and fields:

- CRM: contact profile, tags, referral source, segments and audiences, notes and files, merge duplicates, import and export, blocking, consent and messaging preferences (transactional vs marketing toggles), client ownership.
- Conversations: shared SMS and email inbox on a dedicated number, internal notes and mentions, quick replies, away and after-hours auto-responses, number porting, call recording and voicemail, spam screening, A2P registration.
- Marketing: blast campaigns with audiences and templates, send-time scheduling, resend to unopened, campaign performance, custom sending domain, consent import, discount codes, flash sales, loyalty points and tiers, referral rewards, pixel and analytics tracking.
- Money: checkout, tips, taxes and service charges, multiple tenders, partial and unpaid sales, refunds and voids, receipts, register closeout, gift cards, account credit, memberships with recurring billing and click-to-cancel compliance, packages, retail inventory with suppliers and purchase orders, card readers and processor fees, BNPL, capital, disputes, surcharging (Boulevard Offset), QuickBooks sync.
- Staff and HR: roles and granular permission groups, time clock and timecards, hourly and salary compensation, payroll runs with tax documents, worker self-service.
- Automation: trigger plus filter plus delayed action flows (Mangomint Flows), event-threshold campaigns (Boulevard Fill Slow Days), lifecycle messages (Fresha's 20 types), form-response routing to staff.
- Reporting: sales summary and detail, payments, discounts, staff performance, commissions, inventory, liabilities (gift cards, memberships, account credit), disputes, multi-location rollups, permissions on reports.
- AI: a receptionist agent over calls and texts that reads the calendar, client record and policies, with human handoff and pause controls; content generation for descriptions and marketing copy (Vagaro AI); natural-language questions over reports (Vagaro "Ask Vera").
- Platform: multi-location with per-location pricing and settings, website builder, APIs and integrations, data migration from a prior system.

## What this tells us a template must have

1. A service model with five time components (duration, processing, finishing, buffer before, buffer after) and overrides per staff member, per location and per client; processing time must free the provider for another booking while a resource stays held.
2. A resource model (rooms, chairs, equipment) with groups and capacity, required by services, including services that need a resource but no staff.
3. Availability rules as data: shifts incl. alternating weeks, time off types, closed periods, lead time, booking window, slot interval, daily cap, hidden-slot percentage, and gap-minimizing slot selection.
4. Booking objects beyond the single appointment: multi-service, multi-staff, group, class with capacity, recurring series, requested (approval) bookings, waitlist entries with preference matching and anti-spam alert windows, and an express-booking link that holds a slot until expiry.
5. A policy engine for deposits (percent or fixed), card capture, late-cancel window and fee, no-show fee, full prepayment, auto-cancel if unpaid, per-service and per-client-group overrides, minimum order value, and clear rules for when a fee can be charged and what happens when the card declines.
6. A client wallet: saved cards, account credit, gift card balances, membership entitlements and package units, all redeemable at checkout and visible on the profile.
7. Client record extensions for this family: formulas and treatment notes surfaced at booking and checkout, allergies and patch tests, photo gallery with markup, intake and consent forms with conditional fields, signatures, approval and copy-forward charts, HIPAA mode toggle.
8. Lifecycle messaging as configurable templates per channel (email, SMS, WhatsApp, push) with per-type timing, variables, suppression rules (no rebook reminder if a future booking exists), and metered-cost visibility before sending.
9. A shared inbox on a dedicated number per location with transactional and marketing separation, consent tracking, A2P registration workflow, auto-responses with rate limits, internal notes and mentions.
10. Automation as trigger plus client filter plus delay plus action, with the appointments module publishing triggers (booked, completed, canceled, no-show, nth visit, days since last visit, availability threshold) and generic actions (message, add credit, promo code, notify staff, create task).
11. Checkout that starts from the appointment and supports tips (incl. split and assistant tips), discounts with reasons, taxes and service charges, multiple tenders, partial payments, refunds of single payments and deposits, receipts, prebooking the next visit, and register closeout.
12. Commission and payroll inputs native to the module: service vs product commission, commission swap, tips allocation, backbar deductions, booth rent, time clock, exported to a payroll module or Gusto/QuickBooks.
13. Reports the operators actually use: future bookings, utilization, no-shows and cancellations, rebooking rate and retention per staff, client sales, memberships and gift card liabilities, campaign attribution, with permissions.
14. Multi-location from day one: per-location numbers, pricing, policies, staff sharing, blast rules and reporting rollups, because every competitor prices and configures per location.
15. Discovery integrations: Reserve with Google, Instagram and Facebook booking, website embed and direct links to a specific service or category, Meta Pixel and GA tracking, plus an optional marketplace listing with transparent new-client commission.
16. An AI receptionist on the agent runtime with explicit permissions: read calendar, client notes and policies; book, reschedule, cancel only for the verified caller; hand off to staff; pause per location; and a report comparing AI-handled vs human-handled conversations.
17. Transparent metered costs (texts, WhatsApp, emails, AI minutes) shown inside the product with allowances per plan, because metering is the dominant complaint across all five.
18. Migration tooling: import clients, services, memberships, packages, gift cards and consent records from the prior system, with duplicate merge, since all five advertise free data transfer and users switch often.
