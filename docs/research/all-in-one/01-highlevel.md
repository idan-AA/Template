# HighLevel (GoHighLevel, LeadConnector) feature inventory

Research date: 6 November 2026. Purpose: a user-level inventory of what HighLevel offers today, so a builder can copy the feature surface a small business expects from an all-in-one platform (CRM, conversations, scheduling, marketing, money, automation).

## Method and legend

- Primary sources: help.gohighlevel.com (category, folder and article pages), gohighlevel.com/pricing, ideas.gohighlevel.com (changelog and roadmap), marketplace.gohighlevel.com (developer docs). Secondary sources were used only for pain points and for one older trigger list, and are marked as such.
- About 115 page fetches and 20 searches were run. Many help-center folder pages were read in full (including pagination) to enumerate sub-features. Where a folder had more pages than were read, the row says so.
- OBSERVED = read directly on an official HighLevel page (help center, pricing page, changelog, developer docs). INFERRED = concluded from article titles, folder names, or a third-party page; the row says which. "Not confirmed" means I could not verify it on an official page.
- Tier column: "All" means the pricing page lists the feature under "All Core Features" (available on the $97 Starter plan). Gated features name the plan. "Add-on" means a separately priced usage product.
- The official "A List of Workflow Triggers" article (155000002292) redirects to a Freshdesk login for anonymous visitors, so the trigger list in section 5 was rebuilt from the official per-trigger article folders instead.

Help center URL prefix used below: `https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/` (abbreviated as `HC/` in tables to keep rows readable; `HC/articles/155000002294` means `https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000002294`).

## What this tells us a template must have

1. One tenant record per client business (the "location") with its own contacts, numbers, calendars, pipelines, settings and billing, and a way to stamp a configuration template (snapshot) onto a new tenant without copying customer data.
2. A contact model that is richer than name, email, phone: multiple phones and emails, type, tags, owner, followers, source and attribution, per-channel DND with direction, consent record, engagement score, custom fields including calculated and conditional ones, and companies plus custom objects with associations. Dedupe rules must be a tenant setting.
3. Saved filtered views (smart lists) with typed operators (date relative ranges, string contains, numeric comparisons, DND booleans) and AND/OR nesting; the same filter engine should drive bulk actions, campaign audiences and workflow enrollment.
4. Pipelines as a first-class object: several per tenant, ordered colored stages, Open/Won/Lost/Abandoned statuses, value, owner decoupled from contact owner, stale detection, board and list views, forecasting, bulk edit. Add lost reasons and tag filters since those are HighLevel's top complaints.
5. A unified conversation thread per contact that carries every channel (SMS, MMS, email, calls, WhatsApp, Messenger, Instagram, live chat) with assignment, internal comments with mentions, snippets, drafts, scheduled sends, activity cards, SLA timers, and an AI mode switch (off, suggest, autopilot) per thread.
6. Telephony as a product surface, not just an integration: number purchase, A2P registration flow with status and fee transparency, call recording and transcription, missed call text back, voicemail drops, IVR built in the automation tool, number pools for attribution, per-segment cost visibility.
7. Calendar types that map to how small businesses actually book: one host, round robin team, class with seats, collective, service menu with staff and add-ons, plus resources (rooms, equipment); availability with weekly and date-specific hours, buffers, min notice, date range, slot capacity; deposits at booking; six notification types across email, SMS, in-app and WhatsApp; cancel and reschedule policies; Google and Outlook two-way sync.
8. An automation engine whose trigger and action catalog spans every object (contact, opportunity, company, custom object, appointment, invoice, payment, estimate, document, subscription, coupon, review, form, call, email event, page view, inbound webhook, scheduler) and whose actions include messaging on every channel, record mutation, wait, if/else, goal, split and A/B, go-to, webhooks, Google Sheets, custom code, AI steps (prompt, decision, intent, summarize, translate, extract, agent), and manual tasks for humans. Draft vs published, re-entry rules, stop on response, time windows, timezone mode, per-step stats, enrollment history, execution logs, version history and templates are table stakes.
9. Email marketing with a block builder, brand kit, A/B tests on subject or content with automatic winner, send now / schedule / batch / RSS, segment audiences, resend to unopened, unsubscribe flows, domain warm-up and deliverability diagnostics; SMS campaigns with drip release and compliance guardrails.
10. A sites layer with forms, surveys and quizzes that write directly to contacts (sticky contact, source, consent, payments), a chat widget that fans out to every channel, and funnel or page analytics; full page building can be lighter than HighLevel's if the rest is strong.
11. Reputation as a loop: request (SMS, email, WhatsApp, QR, workflow) with queue statuses, monitor Google and Facebook, sentiment, AI suggested or automatic replies with a drip for the backlog, widgets, and GBP posting.
12. Money end to end: products with one-time and recurring prices, invoices (one-off, recurring with autopay, payment plans, deposits, tips, taxes, attachments, reminders, partial payments), estimates that convert to invoices, documents with e-signature that generate invoices on signing, payment links and text to pay, subscriptions with retries and pause, refunds, coupons with targeting and duration, Tap to Pay, and QuickBooks or Xero sync. Processor-agnostic with a "what works where" matrix.
13. Dashboards built from widgets over any object with date, attribution and UTM filters, plus scheduled PDF custom reports; attribution source captured on every contact and conversion.
14. AI as metered products with clear modes and pricing: per-token conversation agents, per-minute voice agents, per-reply review agents, per-execution workflow AI, included allowances per plan and a wallet that pauses rather than surprises. Fixed-rate rebilling for resellers.
15. Role and permission design with two populations (platform or agency staff vs tenant users), stacked roles, module-level permissions (payments, calendars, pipelines, dashboards, workflows, AI), "login as", audit logs, and a protected owner role.
16. Billing primitives for the operator: prepaid wallet with auto-recharge, usage ledger per tenant, markup or fixed-rate rebilling, resold add-on subscriptions, plan limits on users and contacts, pause and cancel tenant.
17. A mobile experience that covers the daily jobs (inbox, calls, calendar, invoices, payments, tasks, pipeline) rather than a thin notification app.
18. Public API, webhooks, Zapier-style connector and an MCP endpoint from day one, because HighLevel users route around gaps with the API and Reddit threads praise users who could.
19. Avoid HighLevel's pain: a transparent usage and billing screen, deliverability setup wizard with authentication checks, attribution that is correct by default, consistent UI without long-lived Labs forks, and genuinely plug-and-play onboarding for a single business.
